官方题目PDFDMOJ

题目在问什么

在口哨响起的第 s 步，分别算出 Nikky 和 Byron 离起点多远，再输出走得更远的人；距离相同就输出 Tied。

考查知识点

  • 周期分解：每个人按固定的前进步数和后退步数重复行动。
  • 余步模拟：先算完整周期的净移动，再按周期开头的顺序处理剩余步数。
  • 位置比较：题目保证每个周期结束时不落后于起点，所以当前位置就是离起点的距离。

一步一步想

  1. 对一个人，把前进和后退步数相加得到周期长度。divmod(s, 周期长度) 会给出两个数：完整周期数，以及除去这些周期后剩下的步数；后者也就是 s % 周期长度 的结果。
  2. 每个完整周期净前进 forward-backward 步，因此先把这个净移动乘以完整周期数。
  3. 剩余动作先前进，最多 forward 步；之后才后退。将这两部分加到完整周期的净移动上。
  4. 对两个人各算一次位置，然后比较数值，输出 Nikky、Byron 或 Tied。

用例子走一遍

官方样例中，Nikky 每 6 步净前进 2 步，12 步后在 4 的位置。Byron 每 8 步净前进 2 步，12 步包含一个完整周期和 4 个前进步，所以在 6 的位置，答案是 Byron。

为什么这样做是对的

每个完整周期按题目给定的顺序前进 forward 步、后退 backward 步，净位置变化正好是 forward-backward。周期结束后，剩余步数从下一周期的前进阶段开始；min 和 max 两项分别计算剩余的前进与后退。因此函数返回的就是 s 步后的实际位置。由于 forward 不小于 backward，完整周期的净移动非负，周期内后退也不会走过起点，所以比较位置等价于比较离起点的距离。

运行时间与内存

每个人只做固定数量的整数运算，不随 s 增长，所以时间是 O(1)：即使 s 从 12 增加到 10000，运算次数也不会随之增加。额外空间也是 O(1)，因为只保存固定数量的整数。

容易出错的地方

  • 剩余步数超过前进阶段时，超出的部分是后退步，不能把所有剩余步都当成前进。
  • s 恰好是周期长度的倍数时，剩余步数为 0，位置只由完整周期决定。
  • 题目要求比较离起点的距离；这里能直接比较位置，是因为给定条件保证位置不会低于 0。

Python 3 解法

solution.py
import sys


def position(forward, backward, steps):
    cycle_length = forward + backward
    complete_cycles, remaining = divmod(steps, cycle_length)
    distance = complete_cycles * (forward - backward)
    distance += min(remaining, forward)
    distance -= max(0, remaining - forward)
    return distance


a, b, c, d, steps = map(int, sys.stdin.buffer.read().split())
nikky = position(a, b, steps)
byron = position(c, d, steps)

if nikky > byron:
    print("Nikky")
elif byron > nikky:
    print("Byron")
else:
    print("Tied")

Problem statement (English)
Problem J2: Up and Down
Problem Description
Nikky and Byron are playing a silly game in gym class.
Nikky is told by his teacher to walk forward a steps (1 ≤ a ≤ 100) and then walk backward b
steps (1 ≤ b ≤ 100), after which he repeats a forward, b backward, etc. Likewise, Byron is told to
walk forward c steps (1 ≤ c ≤ 100) and then walk backward d steps (1 ≤ d ≤ 100), after which
he repeats c forward, d backward, etc. You may assume that a ≥ b and c ≥ d.
Byron and Nikky have the same length of step, and they are required to take their steps simultane-
ously (that is, Nikky and Byron will both step forward on their first steps at the same time, and this
will continue for each step).
Nikky and Byron start walking from one end of a soccer field. After s steps (1 ≤ s ≤ 10000),
the gym teacher will blow the whistle. Your task is to figure out who has moved the farthest away
from the starting position when the whistle is blown.

Input Specification
The input will be the 5 integers a, b, c, d, and s, each on a separate line.

Output Specification
The output of your program will be one of three possibilities: Nikky if Nikky is farther ahead
after s steps are taken; Byron if Byron is farther ahead after s steps are taken; Tied if Byron and
Nikky are at the same distance from their starting position after s steps are taken.

Sample Input
4
2
5
3
12

Output for Sample Input
Byron

Explanation of Output for Sample Input
Notice that after 12 steps, Nikky has moved 4 − 2 + 4 − 2 steps, for a total of 4 steps from the
starting position, whereas Byron has moved 5 − 3 + 4 steps, for a total of 6 steps from the starting
position. Thus, Byron is ahead.

Official problemPDFDMOJ

What are we asked to do?

Find how far Nikky and Byron are from the start after step s, then print who is farther away or Tied if their distances match.

Knowledge and skills

  • Cycle decomposition: each person repeats a fixed number of forward steps followed by backward steps.
  • Remainder simulation: count the net movement of full cycles, then handle the leftover steps in order.
  • Position comparison: the input guarantees that a person never ends a cycle behind the start, so the position is the distance from the start.

Think step by step

  1. For one person, add the forward and backward counts to get the cycle length. divmod(s, cycle length) returns two numbers: the number of full cycles and the steps left over; that second number is also what s % cycle_length would return.
  2. A full cycle moves forward by forward-backward steps, so multiply that net movement by the number of full cycles.
  3. The leftover actions move forward first, for up to forward steps, and then move backward. Add those movements to the full-cycle result.
  4. Compute both positions and compare them to print Nikky, Byron, or Tied.

Walk through an example

In the official sample, Nikky moves forward by 2 net steps every 6 steps, so after 12 steps he is at position 4. Byron moves forward by 2 net steps every 8 steps; 12 steps contain one full cycle and 4 forward steps, putting him at position 6. The answer is Byron.

Why does this work?

Each full cycle follows the required order of forward then backward steps, for a net change of forward-backward. Any remaining steps begin with the next forward phase; the min and max terms count the remaining forward and backward steps separately. Thus the function returns the actual position after s steps. Since forward is at least backward, a full cycle never has negative net movement, and the backward phase never carries the walker past the start. Comparing positions therefore compares their distances from the start.

Time and memory

Each person uses a fixed number of integer operations, regardless of s, so time is O(1): raising s from 12 to 10000 does not add more operations. Extra space is also O(1), because only a fixed number of integers are stored.

Common mistakes

  • If the remainder is longer than the forward phase, the extra steps are backward steps; do not count them all as forward.
  • When s is an exact multiple of the cycle length, the remainder is zero and only full-cycle movement counts.
  • The problem asks for distance from the start. Position can be compared directly here because the given conditions keep it at or above zero.
